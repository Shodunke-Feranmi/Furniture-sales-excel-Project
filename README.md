# Furniture Sales Analysis Dashboard
 
An Excel-based analysis of four years of furniture sales (2014–2017) covering revenue, profitability, product mix, geography, seasonality, and delivery performance. The workbook ends in an interactive dashboard with KPI cards showing year-over-year change.
 
---
 
## 📌 Project Overview
 
This project analyzes **2,121 order line items** across **1,764 orders** from **707 customers** in the United States. It turns raw transaction data into a dashboard that answers where the business makes money, where it loses it, and when demand peaks.

## 🎯 Business Problem
 
Sales are growing, but profit is not keeping pace. Leadership needs to know which products, regions, and practices drive revenue versus those that quietly erode margin, and when demand peaks so inventory and staffing can be planned.
 
## 📊 Project Objectives
 
- Track sales, profit, and quantity year over year (current year vs. prior year)
- Identify top-performing cities, states, and product categories
- Understand seasonality through monthly sales trends
- Evaluate delivery performance by shipping mode and order-to-ship duration
- Diagnose profitability drivers, including discounting, sub-category, and region

## 📈 Analysis & Key Findings
 
### Overall Performance
- Sales of **$742.0K** produced only **$18.5K** in profit, a **2.5% margin**.
- 2017 sales were **$215.4K, up 8%** on 2016 (**$198.9K**), with quantity up 11%.
- 2017 profit was **$3.0K, down 57%** from **$7.0K** in 2016.
### Product Category
- **Chairs** lead with **$328.4K** in sales (about 44% of the total).
- **Tables** ($207.0K) and **Bookcases** ($114.9K) follow, with **Furnishings** at **$91.7K**.
### Geography
- **California** ($156.1K), **New York** ($93.4K), and **Texas** ($60.6K) are the top states.
- **New York City** ($75.7K) is the top city, followed by **Los Angeles** ($54.0K) and **Seattle** ($41.0K).
### Seasonality
- **December** is the peak month at **$121.8K**, followed closely by **November** ($121.3K) and **September** ($106.4K).
- **February** is the weakest month at **$15.8K**.
### Shipping & Delivery
- **Standard Class** is used for **59%** of orders, followed by Second Class (20%) and First Class (15%).
- Most orders ship within **4 to 5 days** (about 50% of orders).
## 💡 Business Insights
 
1. **The business is growing but not profiting from its growth.**
   Sales rose 8% in 2017 while profit fell 57%, and the margin dropped from 3.5% in 2016 to 1.4%. More volume is not translating into more profit, which points to pricing and cost problems rather than a demand problem.
2. **Discounting is the biggest profit leak.**
   Line items sold at full price earned a **22.7% margin**. Discounts of 20–30% turn margins negative (about **−11%**), and discounts of 30–50% reach about **−39%**. About **34% of all line items lose money**, totaling roughly **$61K in losses**, which erases about 77% of the profit earned on the profitable lines. A discount cap or approval rule would protect margin.
3. **Tables are the problem product; Chairs and Furnishings carry the business.**
   Tables are the second-largest category by sales ($207K) but lose **$17.7K** (−8.6% margin) at an average discount of 26%, and they have lost money every year, worsening in 2017. Chairs generate the most profit ($26.6K) and Furnishings have the best margin (**14.2%**). Reviewing table pricing and supplier costs is the clearest fix.
4. **Sales concentration hides regional losses.**
   California is the largest market (21% of sales) but earns only a 6% margin. The **Central region loses money** (−$2.9K) with the heaviest discounting (about 30%). Texas, Illinois, Pennsylvania, and Ohio are the biggest loss-making states, and top-selling cities such as Philadelphia (−$6.8K) and Houston (−$3.4K) lose money despite strong sales. Regional pricing and discount policy should be reviewed before further investment.
5. **Demand is highly seasonal.**
   September through December generates about **55%** of annual sales, peaking in December, while February is the weakest month. Inventory, staffing, and marketing should be planned for the Q4 surge, with promotions targeted at the slow months instead of blanket discounts.
### Recommendations
- Cap or require approval for discounts above 20%.
- Reprice or renegotiate supplier costs on Tables; review the loss-making products.
- Concentrate growth on Chairs and Furnishings, where margins are healthy.
- Investigate the Central region's discounting practices.
- Prepare inventory and logistics for the September–December peak.
